# Using a reference video for long-form direction

The user may supply a reference video or URL, its transcript, and selected frames or screenshots. Treat these as a creative brief for rhythm, visual explanation, framing, transitions, graphic devices, humor, and emotional pacing. Keep the user's historical source and verified claims separate. A reference transcript or image does not establish a historical fact, a period likeness, map accuracy, motion between stills, or sound that was not supplied.

## Intake and provenance

Save the supplied video, transcript and original frame files unchanged under `source/reference-video/`; record an external video URL if the media is not stored locally. Rename an incoming local video descriptively without transcoding or changing its bytes, and preserve its original name and hash in `source/reference-video/manifest.json`. For each item record a stable ID, kind (`video`, `transcript`, `frame`, or `screenshot`), exact source path or URL, video timecode when known, local SHA-256 when present, what the item visibly or audibly shows, and what remains unknown. Record the user's requested level of influence and any particular moments they want adapted. If only a transcript or stills are supplied, analyze those directly and mark unseen motion, timing and sound as unknown. Do not invent missing frames or timecodes.

**Stage 00 includes a first joint review of the actual video and its transcript.** Probe the video duration, dimensions, frame rate and audio streams; sample the full timeline, then inspect narratively important or ambiguous sections more densely. Read the transcript beside those timecodes. Write `source/reference-video/intake-review.md` with the method and coverage, an observed sequence outline, visual/editing/humor mechanisms, transcript mismatches, historical assertions requiring research, and evidence gaps. State what sampling cannot establish. This review is provisional direction input, not an approved shot plan. If a historical source is not supplied, record `reference_transcript_only` and let Stage 01 independently verify the story before any script approval; the example transcript remains separate from historical citations.

Do not put reference-video material into `source/claims.json` as historical evidence. Normally, a historical detail taken from an example must be verified independently before it appears in narration, a map, on-screen text, or a scene presented as fact. If the user explicitly waives that work for a named episode, preserve the assumption decision and its limits in `source/research-waiver.json` and identify subsequent draft work as based on the reference account, not verified history.

## Direction handoff

In Stage 04, revisit relevant spans of the *actual video* at finer temporal resolution and write `plan/reference-video-analysis.md` alongside `plan/visual-direction.md`. Start from the Stage 00 intake review; for each selected moment, link the manifest item/timecode to (1) the observable technique, (2) the viewer effect, (3) why it fits this episode's verified claim or beat, and (4) an original ChronoStick adaptation. Distinguish a close visual-direction cue from a looser idea. Use the reference to guide the episode's overall feel, including its energy and humor, while keeping approved ChronoStick identity, historical geography, and the named 16:9 profile. A recognizable frame, joke, exact wording, character design, map, or shot sequence is not a generator-ready asset to copy.

Stage 05 must carry selected analysis IDs into the sequence plan and relevant timed shots. Record where each idea changes the framing, cut rhythm, scale, camera, physical action, lighting, visual punchline, map/graphic device, or transition, and why that choice helps the spoken beat. The shot may depart from the reference when historical accuracy, clarity, identity or generation feasibility requires it. Stage 06 makes new approved scene anchors where the adaptation needs them. Stage 08 expresses the *adapted visible result* in pure H3 prompts; prompts do not contain reference-video URLs, analyst notes, or instructions to copy a creator's work. Review the animatic and pilot against the desired reference-guided feel and the actual narrative before bulk generation.

If the user supplies more frames after direction has started, add them to the manifest, revisit only affected analysis IDs, shots, anchors and prompts, and record which downstream decisions changed. Do not silently replace an approved direction or pretend later evidence was present at earlier review.
