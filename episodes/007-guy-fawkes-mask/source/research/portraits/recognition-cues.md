# Fawkes historical-content authority

The archived [Folger detail](gunpowder-conspirators-folger-p281-r001.jpg) depicts
Guido Fawkes third from the right, beneath his name. Folger identifies the print
as Crispijn van de Passe, 1606, ART Box P281 no.7 (size S).
[Institutional description](https://www.folger.edu/blogs/collation/gwu2026-catalog-gunpowder/)
and [catalogue](https://catalog.folger.edu/record/256220).

Visible cues: a broad drooping brim with a relatively plain soft tall crown,
wavy hair reaching beside the cheeks, curling moustache, medium pointed full
beard, pale open collar, buttoned doublet and a draped shoulder cloak. Translate
these into a round off-white head, dot eyes, sparse expression lines and a narrow
costumed stick body. The contemporary engraving's similar faces are probably
stock likenesses, not reliable exact portraits; the
[NPG collection entry](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/portrait/mw00381/The-Gunpowder-Plot-Conspirators-1605)
explicitly records this limitation. Dark brown/charcoal costume colors are
production choices from the approved muted palette, not known portrait colors.

Historical Fawkes never wears the modern mask. The reference animation is a
story/framing input; neutral storyboard costumes are placeholders. Existing
French/Japanese/nineteenth-century people and buildings are unsuitable reuse.
The approved style image is reused only as rendering authority for image design,
never as a production sheet passed to H3.

For Westminster use the [UK Parliament account](https://www.parliament.uk/about/living-heritage/evolutionofparliament/parliamentaryauthority/the-gunpowder-plot-of-1605/overview/the-plot-and-its-discovery/parliament-in-1605/):
medieval buildings; the undercroft was a ground-floor storeroom. Exterior art is
a restrained period evocation, not an exact site reconstruction. No modern clock
tower. The 1807 Smith plan is later evidence, not a contemporary 1605 drawing;
its image could not be downloaded and is not a generation input.
