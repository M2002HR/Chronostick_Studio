## Story

An instantly recognizable mask opens an explosive historical question. Cut to
1605: a Catholic conspiracy targets king and Parliament. Fawkes is guarding the
powder rather than being called the sole mastermind. A warning letter prompts
search; guards catch him before the plan can detonate. The narration records
his later execution, represented by a quiet extinguished-candle insert. Centuries later, the
V for Vendetta mask becomes an emblem of resistance. Return to the opening mask
with an original ironic payoff: the powder failed, the image spread. Append
the tiny spoken `Suscríbete.` over the resolved closing image, within the same
working target. It does not add a text overlay, button or new picture shot.

## Retained and changed

Keep the reference's mask -> historical context -> powder -> arrest -> lasting
idea architecture, emphatic framing, prop inserts and visual contrast. Compress
its long religious explanation and gang roll-call. Replace the platform-game
barrel gag with direct, generator-feasible cellar action. Put the letter before
arrest. Omit graphic punishment, unexplained attribution of the anonymous letter,
modern Big Ben in 1605, joke labels and the exact closing movie quotation.

Maintain a distinction between the real historical Fawkes and the later modern
mask. The historical character never wears a modern Guy Fawkes plastic mask.
A masked contemporary is an anonymous role, not an authentic portrait of Fawkes.
The graphic-novel/modern-symbol passage is independently supported by London
Museum and is made explicit so the title question actually receives an answer.
