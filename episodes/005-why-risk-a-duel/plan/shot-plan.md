# Episode 005 — Why Risk a Duel? — 88-shot rapid-cut plan

## Contract and directing idea

- Approved narration timing: 0.080–52.520 seconds.
- Picture timeline: 11 independent H3 clips × 5.000 seconds = 55.000 seconds.
- Production profile: `h3-short-5s-14step-rapid-cut-55s`.
- Editorial density: exactly eight materially different hard-cut shots in every clip; 88 shots total.
- Rhythm: each cut changes the visual question through subject, scale, angle, prop, light, or event phase. Motion inside every shot stays controlled: one primary action, no more than one camera move, and no more than two subtle secondary movements.
- Ending: narration ends at global 52.520. Clip 11 locks its final handshake tableau by global 52.300 and holds it unchanged through 55.000.
- Style: the entire frame remains inside the locked detailed cinematic ChronoStick world. No photographic backgrounds, realistic anatomy, live-action inserts, contact-sheet layouts, or mixed rendering.
- Generated content: no narration, dialogue, vocal reactions, lip sync, music, subtitles, labels, dates, CTA lettering, or other readable text. The spoken `¡Suscríbete!` remains external narration; editorial CTA typography may be added later.
- Audio: only brief isolated diegetic cues tied to visible actions, with silence between cues. Every later video prompt must include exactly `NO BACKGROUND MUSIC. Natural diegetic sound effects only.`
- Historical scope: the user's source claims are preserved without independent fact-checking. The visuals avoid inventing a named French king, an unsupported French year, named Uruguayan politicians, or readable legal documents.

- The Uruguay sequence uses anonymous politicians and early-1990s material cues without generating the number `1992`; the spoken narration supplies the date.

## Failure conditions

- fewer or more than eight distinct shots in any generated clip
- near-duplicate cuts that do not change framing, subject, scale, prop, or story phase
- frantic body motion, whip pans, camera shake, or more than one camera move in a shot
- duplicate duelists, duplicate Jackson, extra witnesses, extra weapons, or swapped screen direction
- Jackson injured before Clip 06, opponent falling before Jackson fires, or retained bullet moving away from the heart
- French royal ban shown before officer depletion, or Uruguay shown before the final shot of Clip 08
- any named king, named Uruguayan politician, invented date, readable decree, label, subtitle, CTA word, or logo
- blood spray, graphic wound, realistic anatomy, photographic imagery, live-action press footage, or mixed style
- generated speech, crowd voices, lip sync, vocal reaction, continuous ambience, tonal sound bed, or music
- unresolved action or camera motion at any 5.000-second boundary
