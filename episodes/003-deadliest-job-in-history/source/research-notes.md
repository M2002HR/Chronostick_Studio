## Findings

### Fatality-rate denominator

The arithmetic `4 / 46 = 8.6956%` rounds to 8.7%, but `46` is no longer the current ordinal count and is not the number of distinct people who have served. The White House currently identifies Donald Trump as the 45th and 47th president and separately describes 45 presidents across 47 numbered plaques. Therefore:

- four assassinations divided by 47 numbered presidencies is approximately 8.51%
- four assassinations divided by 45 individual presidents is approximately 8.89%
- the approved Spanish narration uses the user-selected distinct-person denominator: `4 / 45`, rounded to 8.9%

Sources: [White House profile of Donald J. Trump](https://www.whitehouse.gov/administration/donald-j-trump/); [White House Presidential Walk of Fame](https://www.whitehouse.gov/walk-of-fame/).

### "Did not survive their presidency"

This phrase is broader than the four assassinations described. Eight vice presidents have succeeded upon a president's death, meaning eight presidents died in office; four of those deaths were assassinations. The episode's actual list is specifically the four assassinated presidents.

Sources: [U.S. Senate history of vice-presidential succession](https://www.senate.gov/about/officers-staff/vice-president/vice-presidents.htm); [National Archives history of presidential protection](https://www.archives.gov/research/jfk/warren-commission-report/appendix7.html).

### Abraham Lincoln

Confirmed: John Wilkes Booth was a prominent actor, a fervent Confederate sympathizer, and Lincoln's assassin. After Robert E. Lee's surrender, Booth changed his kidnapping plot into an assassination plot. Describing his motive only as disliking the Civil War's outcome is a simplified summary, but it is directionally consistent with the documented record.

Source: [National Park Service biography of John Wilkes Booth](https://www.nps.gov/people/john-wilkes-booth.htm).

### James A. Garfield

Confirmed: Charles Guiteau fired two shots; one grazed Garfield's arm or shoulder and the other entered his back. Official historical accounts document serious mental abnormality and behavior consistent with mental illness, although the exact retrospective diagnosis is not settled.

Sources: [National Park Service account of Garfield's shooting](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/the-federal-civil-service-and-the-death-of-president-james-a-garfield.htm); [National Archives profile of Charles Guiteau](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2016/fall/guiteau); [Library of Congress overview](https://www.loc.gov/item/today-in-history/july-02/).

### William McKinley

Confirmed: anarchist Leon Czolgosz shot McKinley at the Pan-American Exposition in Buffalo. He concealed the gun beneath a handkerchief while approaching the president during an afternoon reception at the Temple of Music. "Public exhibition" is understandable but less precise than "Pan-American Exposition."

Source: [National Park Service account of September 6, 1901](https://www.nps.gov/thri/september6.htm).

### John F. Kennedy

Confirmed: Kennedy was shot in Dallas on November 22, 1963, during his presidency and died that day. He was the fourth U.S. president assassinated.

Sources: [National Archives eyewitness exhibit](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/eyewitness/assets/html/14.intro.html); [Warren Commission summary in the National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/jfk/warren-commission-report/chapter-1.html).
