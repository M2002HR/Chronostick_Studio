# Episode 003 visual identity research

## Scope

These notes define recognizable, respectful stick-figure identity cues for the four presidents. They are design inputs, not generator prompts. ChronoStick's approved detailed cinematic stick-world style remains the rendering authority; historical portraits control silhouette, hair, facial hair, clothing period, and distinguishing accessories only.

## Abraham Lincoln

Locked cues:

- exceptionally tall, narrow stick-figure silhouette
- elongated angular face, prominent cheekbones and nose
- short dark chin beard with no mustache
- dark slightly wavy hair
- matte black frock coat, white shirt, black bow tie
- tall black stovepipe hat as the strongest optional silhouette cue

The Library of Congress records a full-length portrait whose description explicitly emphasizes Lincoln's height. National Park Service descriptions identify his elongated angular face, dark hair, chin beard, black suit, white shirt, and bow tie; NPS educational material also treats the black frock coat, top hat, and beard as immediately recognizable iconography.

Sources:

- [Library of Congress — Abraham Lincoln, full-length portrait, standing](https://www.loc.gov/item/2013648779/)
- [National Park Service — White House and President's Park brochure](https://www.nps.gov/whho/planyourvisit/park-brochure.htm)
- [National Park Service — Lincoln at Gettysburg](https://www.nps.gov/gett/learn/education/a-nation-at-war-traveling-trunk-table-6-lincoln-at-gettysburg.htm)
- [National Park Service — Ford's Theatre brochure](https://home.nps.gov/foth/planyourvisit/park-brochure.htm)

## James A. Garfield

Locked cues:

- medium-tall, broader and more solid silhouette than Lincoln
- high forehead with dark hair brushed back and side-parted
- full brown-black beard joined to a full mustache
- dark 1881 frock coat, white shirt, compact black bow tie
- no stovepipe hat in the identity anchor, preserving separation from Lincoln

The Library of Congress presidential portrait collection and Garfield guide provide period portrait authority. A Library of Congress collection description also identifies the beard as medium brown.

Sources:

- [Library of Congress — President James A. Garfield portrait](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/96521555/)
- [Library of Congress — James Garfield resource guide](https://guides.loc.gov/james-garfield)
- [Library of Congress — Garfield correspondence collection presentation](https://crowd.loc.gov/campaigns/garfield-correspondence/july-sept-1880/mss219560127/mss219560127-81/)

## William McKinley

Locked cues:

- compact, upright late-1890s presidential silhouette
- clean-shaven face
- receding dark hair, neatly side-parted
- black frock coat, high white collar, black bow tie
- one small scarlet carnation in the left lapel as the primary color signature

The Library of Congress provides period portrait authority. Smithsonian records identify bow-tie dress and document that McKinley was known to wear red carnations in his buttonhole or lapel.

Sources:

- [Library of Congress — William McKinley portrait](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/89710626/)
- [Library of Congress — William McKinley resource guide](https://guides.loc.gov/william-mckinley)
- [Smithsonian — Ohio scarlet carnation](https://www.si.edu/object/hac_1980.038.003)
- [National Portrait Gallery — William McKinley](https://npg.si.edu/object/npg_NPG.68.56)

## John F. Kennedy

Locked cues:

- youngest and most modern silhouette of the four
- clean-shaven face with a squared jaw
- thick dark chestnut hair, strongly side-parted with a swept front wave
- tailored navy early-1960s suit, white shirt, slim medium-blue tie
- no hat and no period facial hair

The Library of Congress 1961 U.S. Navy portrait is the primary visual authority for head shape, hair mass, clean-shaven face, and early-1960s tailoring.

Sources:

- [Library of Congress — President John F. Kennedy portrait](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/96523447/)
- [Library of Congress — John F. Kennedy portrait, facing slightly left](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/93511088/)

## Cross-character separation test

All four identities must remain distinguishable as black stick silhouettes at thumbnail scale:

1. Lincoln: tallest body + stovepipe hat + chin beard without mustache.
2. Garfield: full beard and mustache + broader 1881 body shape.
3. McKinley: clean-shaven receding hair + scarlet lapel carnation.
4. Kennedy: youthful swept hair + slim modern navy suit and tie.

Do not rely on generated names, labels, captions, plaques, or readable text. Do not turn any identity into a realistic portrait or caricature; the four remain symbolic ChronoStick figures.
