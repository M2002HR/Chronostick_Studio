## Visual grammar

- One unified detailed hand-drawn ChronoStick world: off-white round heads, tiny black eyes, dark clear outlines, simplified stick limbs, period-specific clothes, paper/sand/teal/navy palette. The style authority is `assets/styles/style-detailed-cinematic-stick-history-r001.png`; the 1896 Zanzibar scene anchors translate it to this location.
- Every map is a physical illustrated chart on the same carved table inside the ChronoStick world, and every moving map frame is generated by H3. The world chart shows the British Isles, Germany, eastern Africa, the Indian Ocean and tiny Zanzibar with a stable coastline and three restrained pins. No modern borders or colonial ownership fills appear. Broad Indian Ocean trade paths appear only in trade-context shots. Khalid's miniature maroon token appears only after his introduction. His later movement goes from Zanzibar toward the adjacent mainland area of German East Africa without inventing an exact route. The harbour chart is an explicitly schematic local scale: five British vessels remain west/left, Glasgow separate, palace east/right. Its prebattle, early-battle, sunken-Glasgow and refuge states never reverse or reset.
- Recurring identities: Khalid = black beard, ivory turban, maroon robe with narrow gold edging; Hamoud = short dark beard, ivory turban, ivory robe with deep green sash; Rawson = white beard, navy Victorian officer coat, gold shoulder boards and dark peaked cap. Khalid/Hamoud are consistent illustrated interpretations, not certified portrait likenesses.
- Sound within H3: only isolated short diegetic SFX and silence. The accepted Spanish voice stays separate. No music, dialogue, generated narration or lip sync.
- On-screen writing is restricted to the exact events in `text-events.json`. Each allowed time is on one enamel placard beneath a brass clock inside a single physical scene, generated continuously by H3. Text remains absent from all other anchors and H3 shots. The `09:38` and `38 MINUTOS` reveal images contain the postbattle harbour and appear only after the serious cost passage. The pre-reveal aftermath clock is blank and has no comic character.
- Shot density changes with need: small comic actions in C01, readable maps and place holds in C02, reaction alternation in C03–04, causally ordered action in C05, quiet sustained frames in C06–07. One main action and at most one camera move per five-second shot, ending in a settled frame.
- Battle is readable without turning deaths into spectacle: draw ship recoil, shore impact, rising smoke, retreat and aftermath. No gore. Treat the casualty passage with stable human silhouettes and damaged environment, not a joke.

## Map and text authority

The geographic authority is the Natural Earth 1:10m land GeoJSON saved at `source/map-geometry-natural-earth-10m.geojson`; historical context comes from the 1890 Anglo-German agreement, contemporary map references and Stone Town heritage documentation listed in `plan/map-manifest.json`. The world chart is an original image-generated interpretation inspected for Britain, Germany and Zanzibar placement. The local tactical ship array is schematic: relative sea-to-palace direction, five-to-one ship count and temporal state are the claims, exact bearings and building footprint are not. All selected world and harbour images share physical table, palette and symbol grammar. Still anchors are references only; H3 creates all five-second moving map, clock and text clips. Every visible clock string is registered in the text ledger and requires actual frame-level render review.
