LATE-18TH-CENTURY FRANCE

# Global Environmental Style

All locations must inherit the ChronoStick rendering system.

ENVIRONMENT RENDERING:

- thick clean dark outlines
- simplified but detailed shapes
- controlled soft shading
- restrained historical colors
- readable depth
- limited texture
- strong silhouette separation
- no photoreal material rendering
- no realistic cinematic live-action appearance

Environment detail should be approximately:

MEDIUM TO MEDIUM-HIGH.

The world must feel designed and specific without becoming visually unstable during video generation.

---

# Environment Detail Hierarchy

Every environment should follow a three-layer system.

## Foreground

Foreground may contain one or two storytelling elements.

Examples:

- table edge
- bowl
- potato
- fence
- basket
- document
- sack
- tool
- plant
- doorway edge

Foreground objects should help:

- framing
- depth
- story clarity

Do not overcrowd the foreground.

---

## Midground

The midground is the primary action area.

It contains:

- main characters
- main prop interaction
- major environment object
- central story action

This is the highest-contrast and highest-readability area.

---

## Background

Background should establish historical context without competing with the action.

Background may contain:

- walls
- windows
- buildings
- fields
- roads
- shelves
- trees
- distant people
- carts
- fences
- city architecture

Background contrast must remain lower than the main subject.

---

# Environmental Linework

All major environment objects use visible dark outlines.

Primary outline:

#1C1A18

Environment outline thickness:

slightly thinner than main character outlines.

Important foreground props may use equal outline weight to characters.

Distant architecture may use reduced line weight.

Never use:

- sketch lines
- photographic edges
- painterly blending
- unstable mixed rendering

# Material Language

## Wood

Wood must look:

- matte
- worn
- practical
- simplified

Allowed details:

- broad grain marks
- worn edges
- subtle dents
- muted color variation

Avoid:

- photoreal grain
- glossy varnish
- extreme microtexture

---

## Stone

Stone surfaces should use:

- large simplified shapes
- soft shading
- restrained cracks
- low-frequency texture

Avoid:

- hyper-detailed masonry
- photoreal wall scans

---

## Fabric

Environmental fabric:

- linen
- sack cloth
- apron fabric
- curtain
- simple blanket

Use:

- soft folds
- low-medium detail
- matte shading

---

## Metal

Historical metal objects:

- iron
- simple steel
- buckles
- musket components
- tools

Use:

- small controlled highlights
- simple dark shading

No mirror-like reflections.

---

## Paper

Paper and official documents:

- warm parchment tone
- subtle edge wear
- slight grain
- occasional folds
- wax seal when relevant

Do NOT generate prominent readable body text.

Text will be added later during editing if required.

# Visual Drift Prevention

Every France late-18th-century environment must preserve:

- muted palette
- thick clean outlines
- stylized materials
- simplified historical architecture
- medium environment detail
- non-photoreal rendering
- ChronoStick depth structure

Reject output if:

1. environment becomes photoreal
2. modern objects appear
3. architecture becomes obviously modern
4. rendering changes into 3D mascot style
5. environment becomes flat whiteboard art
6. colors become highly saturated
7. scene becomes excessively cluttered
8. human characters become realistic
9. linework style changes
10. scene no longer matches CST-STYLE-001
