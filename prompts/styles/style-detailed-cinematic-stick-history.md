# Purpose

This file defines the permanent visual language of ChronoStick Studio.

Every character sheet, environment sheet, historical figure design, image prompt, and video prompt must inherit this visual system unless an episode explicitly declares a different approved style version.

The objective is a detailed, cinematic, highly recognizable stick-figure historical animation style.

The style must be visually rich without becoming photorealistic.

Detail must come primarily from:

- historical clothing
- props
- silhouette design
- environments
- lighting
- composition
- historical accessories
- controlled shading

Detail must NOT come from realistic human anatomy, realistic skin, or photorealistic facial rendering.

---

# Core Visual Identity

ChronoStick visuals must look like premium historical animation created specifically for fast-paced YouTube storytelling.

The visual identity combines:

- stylized stick-figure humans
- highly readable silhouettes
- historically detailed clothing
- cinematic composition
- moderately detailed environments
- restrained historical colors
- controlled soft shading
- strong black outlines
- modern short-form visual storytelling

The result must feel:

- polished
- intelligent
- cinematic
- historical
- distinctive
- readable
- expressive

It must NOT feel:

- childish
- generic
- like a whiteboard animation
- like a simple doodle
- photorealistic
- live action
- plastic 3D
- anime
- painterly
- randomly AI-generated

---

# Detail Philosophy

## Primary Rule

DETAIL IN DESIGN.
SIMPLICITY IN HUMAN RENDERING.

The image may contain substantial visual detail, but that detail must be concentrated in controlled areas.

## High Detail Allowed

Use significant detail in:

- historical jackets
- uniforms
- waistcoats
- hats
- belts
- buttons
- scarves
- aprons
- boots
- weapons
- bags
- documents
- tools
- furniture
- architecture
- maps
- food
- fields
- streets
- vehicles appropriate to the historical period
- environmental props

## Medium Detail

Use controlled detail in:

- fabric folds
- simple material shading
- wooden surfaces
- stone walls
- ground texture
- vegetation
- atmospheric depth
- background architecture

## Low Detail

Keep these deliberately simple:

- face
- skin
- hands
- anatomy
- hair strands

The human character must remain obviously stylized.

# Character Geometry

All standard ChronoStick human characters share a common anatomical language.

## Head

Head shape:

- round
- clean
- smooth
- slightly off-white rather than pure digital white

Head diameter is the master proportional unit:

`1 HEAD UNIT = 1.0`

The head should be visually larger than realistic human proportions.

This creates the recognizable ChronoStick silhouette.

## Approximate Proportions

Use these as visual guidance rather than realistic anatomy:

- head diameter: 1.0 unit
- shoulder width: approximately 0.80–0.95 units
- torso length: approximately 1.25–1.45 units
- upper arm: approximately 0.70–0.80 units
- forearm: approximately 0.65–0.75 units
- thigh: approximately 0.90–1.05 units
- lower leg: approximately 0.85–1.00 units

Overall character height should generally be around:

`5.0–5.8 head units`

depending on age, body type, and historical character identity.

Do not use realistic 7–8-head human proportions for standard characters.

---

# Face System

The face must remain deliberately minimal.

## Eyes

Default eyes:

- very small
- black
- simple dots or short shapes

Eyes must never contain:

- realistic iris detail
- realistic reflections
- eyelashes rendered realistically
- complex human eye anatomy

## Eyebrows

Eyebrows may be used extensively for expression.

Use:

- simple black lines
- slight angle changes
- controlled curvature

Eyebrows are one of the primary expression tools.

## Mouth

Use:

- one simple line
- small open oval only when required
- very limited shape vocabulary

Avoid:

- realistic lips
- detailed teeth
- gums
- realistic mouth anatomy

## Nose

Default:

No visible nose.

If a specific character identity absolutely requires a nose cue:

- use only a tiny stylized line or shape
- never realistic nasal anatomy

## Ears

Usually omitted.

If required:

- tiny simplified shapes only

# Real Historical Person System

When representing a real historical person, the character must remain fully inside the ChronoStick visual system.

Do NOT turn the character into a realistic portrait.

Recognition should primarily come from historically documented visual identifiers such as:

- hairstyle
- facial-hair silhouette
- hat
- uniform
- coat
- accessories
- body posture
- recurring objects
- recognizable clothing silhouette
- documented physical presentation
- historically associated visual attributes

The audience should be able to understand which person is being represented without requiring photoreal facial reconstruction.

Each real historical figure must receive an individual locked Character Sheet before repeated use.

Historical identifiers must be researched and verified before that Character Sheet is locked.

Do not invent identifying characteristics.

---

# Body and Limb Style

Limbs must remain simplified and clearly stylized.

Use:

- thin readable arms
- thin readable legs
- simple elbows and knees
- clean silhouettes

Clothing may add volume around the stick anatomy.

The underlying body must still read as a stick-figure character.

Avoid:

- realistic muscular anatomy
- realistic shoulder anatomy
- detailed veins
- realistic hands
- detailed fingernails
- hyper-articulated joints

---

# Hands

Hands must remain simplified.

Default hand system:

- mitten-like simplified form
- or very simple fingers when interaction clarity requires them

Maximum useful detail:

- enough to hold an object clearly
- enough to point
- enough to gesture

Never render realistic human hands.

---

# Hair

Hair must be shape-based.

Use:

- solid graphic masses
- a few controlled silhouette details
- historically appropriate shapes

Avoid:

- thousands of individual strands
- photorealistic hair
- glossy hair simulation

Hair exists primarily to support:

- identity
- period accuracy
- silhouette recognition

---

# Clothing System

Historical clothing is one of the primary sources of visual richness.

Clothing should contain substantially more detail than the underlying body.

Allowed:

- clear layers
- buttons
- belts
- cuffs
- collars
- straps
- pockets
- simplified stitching
- fabric folds
- worn edges when appropriate
- historically appropriate accessories

Clothing must maintain:

- strong silhouette
- readable color blocking
- clean outlines

Avoid excessive micro-detail that may cause instability during video generation.

---

# Outline System

Outlines are mandatory.

Primary outline color:

`#1C1A18`

Outlines should be:

- dark
- clean
- confident
- consistent
- clearly visible

Relative outline thickness:

approximately 1.5%–2.5% of the character head diameter.

Important objects may use slightly heavier outlines.

Background objects may use slightly thinner outlines.

Never use:

- sketchy pencil lines
- unstable broken outlines
- photoreal edge rendering

# Palette Distribution

As general guidance:

- 55–65% neutral parchment / beige / brown
- 15–25% muted blues and navy
- 10–15% darker neutral tones
- 5–10% burgundy or olive accents
- less than 5% gold or special accent

Do not make every scene identical.

Maintain the palette family while allowing appropriate scene variation.

---

# Saturation

Overall saturation:

LOW TO MEDIUM.

Avoid:

- neon colors
- digital cyan
- bright modern blue
- hot pink
- electric green
- candy colors
- superhero saturation

Historical environments should feel warm, grounded, and slightly desaturated.

---

# Shading System

Shading must remain controlled.

Use:

- soft directional shadows
- simple form shading
- limited gradient transitions
- subtle ambient shadow
- restrained highlights

Characters may have enough shading to appear dimensional without becoming realistic.

Do NOT use:

- realistic skin subsurface scattering
- highly glossy surfaces
- hyper-real lighting
- complex physically based skin rendering
- excessive volumetric effects

---

# Material Rendering

## Fabric

Fabric should show:

- simplified folds
- soft matte shading
- limited texture

## Wood

Wood may show:

- broad grain indication
- worn edges
- matte surface

Avoid photoreal microtexture.

## Metal

Metal may have:

- simple controlled highlight
- slightly darker shadow
- readable metallic identity

Avoid mirror-like reflections.

## Paper

Paper should have:

- warm parchment tone
- subtle grain
- slightly worn edges when appropriate

Do not generate unnecessary readable text.

---

# Environment Philosophy

Environments must never be empty.

Environments must also never overpower the characters.

Each scene should contain enough historical context to immediately establish:

- place
- social class
- period
- situation

without becoming cluttered.

---

# Environment Detail Hierarchy

## Foreground

May contain:

- one or two storytelling objects
- table edge
- fence
- paper
- food
- vegetation
- tool

Foreground elements may assist depth.

## Midground

Contains:

- main characters
- main action
- major props

This is the visual priority.

## Background

Contains simplified contextual information:

- architecture
- furniture
- fields
- distant people
- roads
- windows
- walls
- trees

Background detail must remain subordinate.

---

# Background Complexity

Target:

MEDIUM DETAIL.

Not:

empty flat-color background.

Not:

dense photoreal environment.

Background should typically use:

- larger shapes
- controlled linework
- simplified texture
- lower contrast than main characters

---

# Depth

Use three readable depth layers when useful:

1. foreground
2. subject plane
3. background

Use:

- scale difference
- controlled overlap
- subtle atmospheric value change

Avoid extreme cinematic depth-of-field blur.

A small amount of stylized focus separation is acceptable.

---

# Lighting System

Lighting should appear cinematic but remain animation-friendly.

## Day Exterior

Use:

- soft directional daylight
- gentle shadows
- warm-neutral highlights
- readable faces and costumes

## Interior Day

Use:

- window light
- warm reflected light
- modest contrast

## Candle / Night Interior

Use:

- warm amber key light
- dark navy or muted gray shadows
- clearly readable character silhouette

## Night Exterior

Use:

- deep muted navy environment
- soft moonlight
- warm lantern accent if historically appropriate
- characters must remain readable

Do not make night scenes so dark that details disappear.

---

# Character Lighting Rule

The character's head must remain clearly distinguishable from the environment.

Do not allow a white head to disappear into bright backgrounds.

Use:

- outline
- background value separation
- controlled shadow

---

# Cinematic Composition

The style should feel cinematic without imitating live-action realism.

Preferred framing:

- medium shot
- medium close-up
- close-up
- wide establishing shot when necessary
- occasional extreme close-up for hooks

Avoid unnecessary ultra-wide distortion.

---

# Vertical 9:16 Composition

Primary output:

`9:16 vertical`

Critical subjects should remain primarily inside the central safe region.

As general guidance:

- keep essential subjects within central ~80% of frame width
- keep essential facial information away from extreme top and bottom
- leave some clean space when captions will later be added

Do not place critical storytelling information only at the far-right or extreme bottom edge.

---

# Camera Movement

Camera movement must be controlled.

Preferred:

- gentle push-in
- gentle pull-back
- short lateral pan
- subtle vertical reveal
- small reframing
- static camera with character movement

Use aggressive camera motion only when deliberately specified for a particular hook beat.

Avoid:

- uncontrolled spinning
- orbiting around characters
- extreme handheld shake
- impossible camera motion
- constant zooming
- rapid perspective deformation

---

# Motion Philosophy

Animation motion level:

MODERATE.

Characters must feel alive.

They must not feel frozen.

However, each shot should generally contain:

- one primary character action
- zero to two subtle secondary movements

Examples of secondary motion:

- subtle breathing
- small head turn
- cloth movement
- slight hand adjustment
- small background motion

Do not make every object move simultaneously.

---

# Character Motion

Preferred:

- look toward object
- turn head
- point
- take one or two steps
- sit
- stand
- pick up object
- push object away
- hold object
- exchange glance
- simple reaction

Avoid unnecessarily complicated choreography.

---

# Video Shot Editing

Cuts should happen inside generated video clips.

The animation should feel professionally edited rather than like one continuous AI shot.

## Hook Segments

The opening hook may contain:

`5–7 distinct visual beats/shots within 10 seconds`

Typical shot duration:

`0.6–1.8 seconds`

Cuts should be:

- hard
- clean
- purposeful

The hook may use faster pacing than the rest of the episode.

## Standard Body Segments

Typical:

`3–5 shots within 10 seconds`

Typical shot duration:

`1.3–3.0 seconds`

The storytelling should remain energetic but readable.

## Ending / CTA Segment

Typical:

`2–4 shots within 10 seconds`

Allow slightly longer readable shots near the final CTA.

---

# Cut Philosophy

Preferred:

- hard cut
- reaction cut
- close-up cut
- insert shot
- match-on-action when entirely inside one generated clip

Use transitions sparingly.

Avoid:

- excessive dissolves
- dreamy transitions
- constant whip-pans
- unnecessary morph transitions

---

# Absolute 10-Second Boundary Rule

This is a permanent ChronoStick production rule.

Each independently generated 10-second video must be visually self-contained.

The final shot must be fully resolved BEFORE the generation ends.

Target final resolution window:

`9.2–10.0 seconds`

During this period:

- complete the current action
- settle the camera
- arrive at a readable final composition

Never create:

- unfinished camera movement
- half-completed character action
- a transition requiring the next generated video
- a match-frame dependency between two separate generations

The next generated 10-second video starts as a completely new shot or scene.

Audio narration and background music may continue uninterrupted across the cut.

Visual continuity across separately generated videos is NOT required.

Editorial continuity is achieved through:

- narration
- music
- story logic
- intentional hard cuts

---

# Style Drift Prevention

Every generated visual must preserve:

- round off-white heads
- minimal faces
- black outlines
- simplified anatomy
- historical clothing detail
- muted palette
- controlled shading
- consistent environment language

If any shot becomes:

- realistic human
- detailed human skin
- CGI mascot
- anime
- flat doodle
- photoreal environment with realistic actor
- painterly illustration

the shot is considered FAILED.

---

# Character Consistency Rule

Recurring characters must use an approved Character Sheet reference whenever technically possible.

The model must not independently redesign:

- hairstyle
- head shape
- face system
- outfit colors
- outfit structure
- signature accessories
- body proportions

unless explicitly instructed by an approved character-version update.

---

# Historical Identity Rule

For real historical figures:

Recognition must come from a controlled combination of verified historical identifiers.

Possible identifiers include:

- silhouette
- hair
- facial-hair shape
- historically documented clothing
- uniform
- headwear
- recurring accessories
- posture
- known visual attributes

Never depend entirely on photoreal facial similarity.

Character identity must remain readable inside the ChronoStick system.

Each important real historical person receives their own Character Sheet.

---

# Text Inside Generated Visuals

Default rule:

DO NOT generate readable text inside image/video generations.

Reasons:

- generated text may be incorrect
- typography may drift
- localization may change

Add:

- captions
- names
- dates
- labels
- CTA text

later inside the editing environment.

Exceptions require explicit instruction.

---

# Historical Symbols

Historical symbols may appear when they are factually relevant to the scene.

They should be:

- contextual
- historically appropriate
- visually controlled

Do not add unrelated political, military, religious, or national symbols merely for decoration.

---

# Prop Consistency

Recurring important props should maintain:

- approximate shape
- palette
- design language

Examples:

- potatoes
- bowls
- bags
- maps
- weapons
- books
- documents

Props associated with a recurring character may become part of that character's Character Sheet.

---

# Style Failure Conditions

Reject a generation if any of the following occurs:

1. Human becomes photorealistic.
2. Head changes from the approved stick-figure head system.
3. Facial anatomy becomes realistic.
4. Character clothing changes without instruction.
5. Historical period becomes visually incorrect.
6. Modern objects appear unintentionally.
7. Outline language disappears.
8. Palette becomes brightly saturated.
9. Environment becomes photoreal while characters remain stylized.
10. Character proportions visibly drift.
11. A video ends during an unfinished action.
12. Separate 10-second clips rely on matching boundary frames.
13. Motion becomes chaotic enough to damage character consistency.
14. Generated text becomes visually prominent without being requested.
